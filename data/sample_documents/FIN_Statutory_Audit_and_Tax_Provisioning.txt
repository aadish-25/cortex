1. ADVANCE TAX QUARTERLY PAYMENT SCHEDULES (INCOME TAX ACT, 1961)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Statutory Liability Framework:
    Under Section 208 and 209 of the Indian Income Tax Act, 1961, the Company is
    statutorily required to deposit corporate advance tax in four mandatory quarterly
    tranches whenever estimated annual net income tax liability exceeds ₹10,000 INR.

1.2 Mandatory Advance Tax Quarterly Due Dates & Cumulative Thresholds:
    Pursuant to Section 211(1) of the Income Tax Act:

    ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
    INSTALLMENT DUE DATE            CUMULATIVE STATUTORY TAX PAYABLE
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
    On or before 15th June          Not less than 15% of estimated net advance tax
    On or before 15th September     Not less than 45% of estimated net advance tax (less tax paid earlier)
    On or before 15th December      Not less than 75% of estimated net advance tax (less tax paid earlier)
    On or before 15th March         100% of estimated net advance tax (less tax paid earlier)
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------------

1.3 Penal Interest Avoidance under Section 234B & 234C:
    - Section 234C imposes simple interest at 1% per month on deferment of advance tax
      installments if paid amounts fall short of the cumulative percentages above.
    - Section 234B imposes interest at 1% per month if advance tax paid before 31st
      March is less than 90% of assessed tax.
    - The Corporate Taxation Desk must complete quarterly closing provisions and
      challan deposits via the Income Tax e-filing portal (e-Pay Tax) at least 48 hours
      prior to each statutory cutoff date.

2. TRANSFER PRICING DOCUMENTATION (SECTION 92E OF THE INCOME TAX ACT)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Statutory Transfer Pricing Requirement:
    Cortex Technologies India Private Limited engages in significant international
    transactions and cross-border software development, R&D technical support,
    and IP licensing with its overseas corporate affiliates and holding entities.

2.2 Arm's Length Price (ALP) Benchmarking:
    - Pursuant to Sections 92C and 92D of the Income Tax Act, 1961 read with Rule 10D
      of the Income Tax Rules, all intercompany transactions must satisfy the Arm's
      Length Standard.
    - The Company adopts the Transactional Net Margin Method (TNMM) as the primary
      Most Appropriate Method (MAM) utilizing the Operating Profit to Total Cost (OP/TC)
      profit level indicator (PLI).
    - Transfer pricing mark-up on cost-plus software development services is benchmarked
      at an arm's length range of 15% to 18% over operating expenditures.

2.3 Mandatory Accountant's Report (Form 3CEB):
    - Under Section 92E, the Company must obtain and electronically file a certified
      Accountant's Report in **Form No. 3CEB** from an independent Chartered Accountant
      on or before October 31 following the relevant assessment year.
    - Failure to furnish Form 3CEB attracts a statutory penalty of ₹1,00,000 under
      Section 271BA, plus penalties of 2% of the transaction value under Section 271AA
      for failure to maintain mandated TP documentation.

3. STATUTORY EXTERNAL AUDIT BY BIG 4 AUDITORS (COMPANIES ACT, 2013)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Appointment & Auditor Independence:
    - Pursuant to Section 139 of the Indian Companies Act, 2013, the Company's statutory
      external audit is conducted exclusively by an empanelled **Big 4 Audit Firm**
      (e.g., Deloitte Haskins & Sells, Ernst & Young / SR Batliboi, PwC / Price Waterhouse,
      KPMG / BSR & Co.) appointed by the shareholders at the Annual General Meeting (AGM).
    - Mandatory audit partner rotation is strictly enforced every five (5) consecutive
      financial years under Section 139(2).

3.2 Scope of Audit & Regulatory Compliance:
    The external auditors conduct audits in compliance with:
    a) Standards on Auditing (SAs) issued by the Institute of Chartered Accountants
       of India (ICAI) and overseen by the National Financial Reporting Authority (NFRA);
    b) Companies (Auditor's Report) Order, 2020 (CARO 2020) reporting requirements,
       including verification of inventory, tangible assets, internal audit systems,
       and statutory dues (PF, ESI, GST, TDS);
    c) Section 143(3)(i) of the Companies Act, 2013 regarding adequacy and operating
       effectiveness of Internal Financial Controls over Financial Reporting (IFC-FR).

3.3 Non-Audit Services Prohibition:
    Pursuant to Section 144 of the Companies Act, 2013, the statutory external audit
    firm is strictly barred from providing internal audit, accounting, actuarial,
    investment banking, or outsourced management services to the Company.

4. CAPITAL EXPENDITURE (CAPEX) DELEGATION OF AUTHORITY (DoA) IN INR
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Monetary Authorization Limits:
    Every Capital Expenditure Request (CER) must adhere to the following approval matrix:

    ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
    EXPENDITURE VALUE RANGE (INR)       MANDATORY APPROVAL AUTHORITIES
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------------
    Up to ₹5,00,000 INR                 Department Head & Financial Controller
    ₹5,00,001 to ₹25,00,000 INR         VP of Department & Head of Finance
    ₹25,00,001 to ₹1,00,00,000 INR      Chief Financial Officer (CFO) [MANDATORY]
    Exceeding ₹1,00,00,000 INR (1 Cr+)  Chief Executive Officer (CEO) AND
                                        Board of Directors Audit Committee Co-Sign
    ----------------------------------------------------------------------------

4.2 Invoicing and PO Compliance:
    Splitting purchase orders or capital contracts to circumvent the ₹25,00,000 CFO
    threshold or ₹1 Crore Board threshold constitutes severe financial non-compliance
    resulting in immediate audit investigation and potential disciplinary termination.
