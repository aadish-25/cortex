SECTION 1: STATUTORY COMPLIANCE & THE POSH ACT, 2013
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
1.1 Statutory Mandate:
    Cortex Technologies India Private Limited enforces absolute compliance with the
    Sexual Harassment of Women at Workplace (Prevention, Prohibition and Redressal)
    Act, 2013 ("POSH Act"). The Company maintains a strict zero-tolerance policy
    toward any form of sexual harassment, unwelcome physical contact, sexually
    colored remarks, demand or request for sexual favors, showing pornography, or
    any unwelcome physical, verbal, or non-verbal conduct of a sexual nature.

1.2 Extended Workplace Scope:
    Pursuant to Section 2(o) of the POSH Act, "workplace" encompasses:
    a) All corporate office locations in Bengaluru, Mumbai, Gurugram, Hyderabad, and Pune;
    b) Any location visited by the employee arising out of or during the course of
       employment, including technical offsites, conferences, and transit;
    c) Virtual digital workspaces, including Slack channels, Zoom meetings, GitHub PR
       discussions, enterprise emails, and company social networking platforms;
    d) Company guest houses, partnered hotels, and company-sponsored transport (cabs).

SECTION 2: CONSTITUTION OF THE INTERNAL COMPLAINTS COMMITTEE (ICC)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
2.1 Committee Architecture (Section 4 of POSH Act):
    The Company has constituted a permanent Internal Complaints Committee (ICC)
    at every registered administrative facility. The ICC is composed of:
    a) Presiding Officer: A woman employed at a senior managerial level (VP or Director);
    b) Employee Members: Not less than two employee members preferably committed to
       the cause of women or who have experience in social work or legal knowledge;
    c) External Member: One independent member from a recognized Non-Governmental
       Organization (NGO) or association committed to women's rights or a legal
       expert conversant with sexual harassment issues;
    d) Gender Composition: At least one-half (50%) of the total appointed members
       of the ICC must be women at all times.

2.2 Current ICC Leadership:
    - Presiding Officer: Dr. Radhika Nair (VP, People & Culture, Bengaluru Hub)
    - External Counsel Member: Adv. Meenakshi Sundaram (High Court of Karnataka / Human Rights Forum)
    - Member Registry & Conciliation Desk: `icc-helpline@cortex.internal`

SECTION 3: INQUIRY PROCESS & STATUTORY TIMELINES (SLA)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
3.1 Complaint Filing Window:
    An aggrieved person may file a formal written complaint with the ICC within a
    period of three (3) months from the date of the incident (extendable by up to
    another three months if the ICC is satisfied that circumstances prevented earlier filing).

3.2 Mandatory 90-Day Inquiry Completion SLA:
    - Pursuant to Section 11 of the POSH Act, the ICC must initiate and conclude its
      formal inquiry within a strict statutory timeframe of **ninety (90) calendar days**
      from the date of receipt of the formal complaint.
    - Principles of natural justice and fair opportunity to be heard will be accorded
      to both the complainant and the respondent.
    - The ICC holds the same powers as vested in a Civil Court under the Code of Civil
      Procedure, 1908, when summoning persons, examining witnesses under oath, and
      demanding discovery or production of documents.

3.3 Report Submission & Employer Implementation:
    - Within ten (10) calendar days of concluding the inquiry, the ICC must submit its
      formal inquiry findings and recommendations report to the Executive Board and
      the District Officer (DO).
    - The Company must implement the recommended disciplinary or remedial action
      within sixty (60) calendar days of receipt of the ICC report.

3.4 Strict Confidentiality & Penalty for Disclosure (Section 16):
    The identity and address of the aggrieved woman, respondent, and witnesses, as
    well as any inquiry proceedings and recommendations, must not be published,
    communicated, or made known to the public, press, or media in any manner. Any
    person breaching confidentiality is subject to a statutory monetary penalty of
    ₹5,000, immediate disciplinary removal, and legal prosecution.

SECTION 4: INTERIM RELIEF & DISCIPLINARY SANCTIONS
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
4.1 Interim Relief Measures:
    During the pendency of an ICC inquiry, upon written request of the complainant,
    the ICC may recommend:
    a) Transfer of the complainant or the respondent to another department or city office;
    b) Grant of paid leave to the complainant up to a period of three (3) months
       (in addition to regular leave entitlements);
    c) Restraining the respondent from reporting on or evaluating the complainant's performance.

4.2 Disciplinary Actions upon Substantiated Harassment:
    Depending on the severity of the offense, sanctions include:
    a) Written reprimand and formal censure placed permanently on HR records;
    b) Withholding of promotions, increments, or performance-linked bonuses;
    c) Immediate termination of employment for gross misconduct without notice;
    d) Monetary deduction from salary/wages for compensation payable to the victim;
    e) Criminal complaints filed with jurisdictional police stations under the
       Bharatiya Nyaya Sanhita (BNS) / relevant criminal statutes.

--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
SECTION 5: WHISTLEBLOWER INTEGRITY & ANTI-RETALIATION SHIELD
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
5.1 Protected Disclosures:
    Employees may report unethical conduct, financial fraud, corruption, bribery,
    or statutory non-compliance through the Confidential Whistleblower Hotline
    (`whistleblower@cortex.internal` or anonymous portal).

5.2 Anti-Retaliation Policy:
    Cortex strictly prohibits any form of retaliation, adverse performance review,
    threat of termination, demotion, transfer, or informal harassment against any
    employee who in good faith reports a violation, files a POSH complaint, or
    participates as a witness in an ICC inquiry.
    Any managerial retaliation is treated as a severe independent violation warranting
    immediate dismissal.
